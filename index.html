Overview

Video models today are largely reactive. They respond only when asked, on clips that have already ended. Real-world systems such as wearable assistants, robots, and autonomous vehicles must instead watch an unbounded stream and decide when to speak, warn, or act. This workshop brings together researchers in egocentric, robotic, and streaming video understanding to study proactivity as a core capability: knowing when to interrupt, anticipating human intent from video and audio, ensuring proactive safety in real time, and explaining decisions through interactive visuals. The program features invited talks, a peer-reviewed paper track, a competition, and a panel on proactive AI.

Topics

1

AI that knows when to interrupt

When to speak or stay silent in streaming video-language models: timing, turn-taking, false alarms vs. missed interventions.

2

Understanding human intent through video and audio

Anticipating goals, next actions, and needs from egocentric and third-person video, speech, and sound.

3

Proactive safety in real-time AI

Hazard anticipation and early warning in robotics, driving, and wearables; safe intervention policies.

4

AI that explains through interactive visuals

Grounded explanations (regions, trajectories, future states) that keep humans in the loop.

Competition

A challenge on proactive memory in streaming egocentric video, built on the ProactEgoMem benchmark. A system watches an egocentric video stream without any user query and, from inferred user intent, decides on its own when to act and what to remember. Participation is open to academic and industrial teams.

  • Tasks: Information Capture (preserve transient informative details) · State Tracking (follow object states) · Location Tracking (record object placements)
  • Levels: Notification (send one timely message) · Intervention (maintain a background memory tool to proactively acquire memory)
  • Data: 400 egocentric video clips (22.5 hours) in two tracks: Daily (300 clips from Ego4D, 1,000 questions) · Science (100 laboratory clips from FineBio, BioVL-QR, and Ego4D, 500 questions). Fifty development clips are released with labels.
  • Evaluation: Held-out test set, scored with a standard evaluation script on an evaluation server with a public leaderboard. Metrics: timing (Start@0.2), detection (precision, recall, F1), lifecycle overlap (mIoU), and QA accuracy from each system's proactive memory.
  • Rules: At most three submissions per team, and one team per institution. Finalists provide code or a reproducible description of their method.

Hosted on CodaBench (evaluation server and public leaderboard) In preparation

Top entries present their methods in a dedicated results session, and winning teams are invited to submit a technical report.
