Overview

Video models today are largely reactive: they respond only when asked, on clips that have already ended. Real-world systems such as wearable assistants, robots, autonomous vehicles must instead watch an unbounded stream and decide on their own when to speak, warn, or act. This workshop brings together researchers in egocentric, robotic, and streaming video understanding to study proactivity as a core capability: knowing when to interrupt, anticipating human intent from video and audio, ensuring proactive safety in real time, and explaining decisions through interactive visuals. The program features invited talks, a peer-reviewed paper track, a competition track, and a panel on how to evaluate proactive video AI.

Topics

1

AI that knows when to interrupt

When to speak or stay silent in streaming video-language models: timing, turn-taking, false alarms vs. missed interventions.

2

Understanding human intent through video and audio

Anticipating goals, next actions, and needs from egocentric and third-person video, speech, and sound.

3

Proactive safety in real-time AI

Hazard anticipation and early warning in robotics, driving, and wearables; safe intervention policies.

4

AI that explains through interactive visuals

Grounded explanations (regions, trajectories, future states) that keep humans in the loop.

Competition

A challenge on proactive memory in streaming egocentric video on wearable devices, built on the ProactEgoMem benchmark. A system watches an egocentric video stream one second at a time, receives no user query, and decides on its own when to act and what to remember.

  • Tasks: Information Capture · State Tracking · Location Tracking
  • Levels: Notification (one timely message) · Intervention (run a background memory tool, then answer questions from memory)
  • Tracks: ProactEgoMem-Daily (Ego4D) · ProactEgoMem-Sci (lab recordings)
  • Ranking: ProactEgoMem score: the mean of Notification F1, Intervention mIoU, and QA accuracy, averaged over the three tasks and both tracks. Scored on a held-out test set through an evaluation server with a public leaderboard.

Top-ranked teams present at the workshop and are invited to submit a technical report.
